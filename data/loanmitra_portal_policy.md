# Who decides an application and how pre-qualification works

> **Provenance note:** This is LoanMitra portal policy written for a student demonstration portal. It describes how this portal handles applications for HDFC Bank Business Loan, Personal Loan and Credit Card products. It is not HDFC Bank's internal credit policy and does not bind HDFC Bank.

## A person makes every final decision

Every application on the LoanMitra portal is decided by a person. The automated pre-qualification check only sorts applications: it can pass an application to a loan officer, send a borderline application for manual review, or decline it at the pre-qualification stage. Approval or rejection of an application that reaches review is always made by a loan officer, who must record a written reason. The AI assistant and the AI-generated case summary never approve, reject, score or change an application. They only explain information that already exists, and they can make mistakes, so officers check them against the application record.

## How the pre-qualification score is calculated

Pre-qualification is a fixed set of rules for each product, applied the same way to every applicant. Each rule either passes or fails and carries a number of points, and the points of the passed rules add up to a score out of 100. A score of 70 or more passes pre-qualification and the application goes to a loan officer. A score from 50 to 69 is borderline and goes to manual review, where a loan officer looks at it more closely. A score below 50 is declined at the pre-qualification stage. The same inputs always give the same score; nothing in the calculation is random or based on a model's opinion.

## Hard eligibility requirements (knockout rules)

Some rules are hard eligibility requirements, also called knockout rules. If any knockout rule fails, the application fails pre-qualification even if the total score is high, because the applicant does not meet a basic condition of the product. For example, a personal loan applicant who scores 95 points but is self-employed still fails, because the personal loan on this portal is offered to salaried applicants only. A credit score below 550 is a hard requirement for every product. Scored rules, by contrast, only reduce the total: failing one scored rule, such as a credit score between 550 and 649, usually moves an otherwise strong application into manual review rather than declining it.

## Rules for each product

For a Business Loan, the requested amount must be between ₹1 lakh and ₹5 crore (hard requirement); the credit score should be 650 or more (25 points); the business should have operated for at least 2 years (20 points); annual revenue should be at least twice the loan amount (20 points); and the estimated EMI should fit within the monthly EMI the applicant says the business can pay (25 points). For a Personal Loan, the hard requirements are an amount from ₹25,000 to ₹50 lakh, age 21 to 60, salaried employment, at least 2 years of total work experience including 1 year with the current employer, and net monthly income of at least ₹25,000; the credit score should be 650 or more (35 points) and fixed obligations should stay within 50% of income (35 points). For a Credit Card, the hard requirements are age 18 or above and net monthly income of at least ₹25,000; the credit score should be 650 or more (35 points) and existing EMIs should stay within 50% of income (35 points).

## Where the thresholds come from

For the Personal Loan, the hard limits on age, minimum income, salaried employment and loan amount follow HDFC Bank's publicly listed eligibility criteria. The Business Loan rules on this portal are simplified portal thresholds and are looser than HDFC Bank's published Business Loan criteria, which list a minimum annual turnover of ₹40 lakh, at least three years in the current business and loan amounts of up to ₹75 lakh; an applicant who passes the portal rules may still not meet HDFC Bank's own criteria. The 650 credit score threshold and the 50% limit on fixed obligations are LoanMitra portal policy, because HDFC Bank does not publish a single credit score cut-off or obligation ratio for all applicants. The estimated EMI used for affordability checks assumes 12% per annum over 36 months; the rate actually offered on a loan is decided by the lender at sanction.

## Self-reported information is marked unverified

The pre-qualification check runs on the figures the applicant typed into the form: income, existing EMIs, revenue, years in business, work experience and credit score. Until documents are checked, these values are self-reported, and the rule results mark them as unverified. A pass at pre-qualification is therefore not a loan approval. The loan officer compares the declared figures with the uploaded documents before deciding, and a mismatch between the form and the documents is a valid reason to reject or to ask the applicant for clarification.

## A changed credit score does not update a submitted application

The score and rule results are worked out once, at the moment the application is submitted, and stored with the application. If the applicant's credit score, income or debts change afterwards, the stored result does not change by itself, and a submitted application can no longer be edited. To have new figures considered, the applicant can start a new application with the updated information, or, if the application failed pre-qualification, mention the change with supporting evidence in an appeal. A loan officer can re-run pre-qualification, but the re-run uses the figures already on the application.

# Drafts, submission, statuses, withdrawal and reapplying

> **Provenance note:** LoanMitra portal policy for a student demonstration portal; not HDFC Bank policy.

## Completing a draft before submission

An application stays a draft until the applicant submits it, and a draft can be edited as often as needed. Submission is refused until every required field for the chosen product is filled in and every required document is uploaded. A Business Loan needs a Photo ID, a Bank Statement and a Business Registration Certificate. A Personal Loan needs a Photo ID, a Salary Slip and a Bank Statement. A Credit Card needs a Photo ID and a Bank Statement. A GST Certificate, ITR or other supporting document may be added but is not required. Documents can be uploaded only while the application is a draft, as PDF, JPG or PNG files of up to 10 MB each. Once submitted, the application can no longer be edited and pre-qualification runs immediately.

## What each status means and typical timelines

After submission an application shows one of these statuses. Pre-qualification passed means it has been sent to a loan officer; an officer usually picks it up within a few working days. Manual review means the score was borderline or the applicant appealed a failed result, and a loan officer will look at it more closely, also usually within a few working days. Under review means a loan officer is working on it now; a decision usually takes 4 to 7 working days. Approved and rejected are final decisions made by a loan officer. Pre-qualification failed means the application did not meet the rules and will not reach an officer unless the applicant appeals. Withdrawn means the applicant cancelled it. These timelines are the portal's service targets, not guarantees. The applicant receives an email at every status change.

## Withdrawing or cancelling an application

An applicant can withdraw an application at any time before a loan officer makes a final decision, including while it is a draft, after pre-qualification, in manual review or under review. Withdrawal is permanent: a withdrawn application cannot be reopened, resumed or restored. To continue, the applicant starts a new application from the Apply page. Approved and rejected applications cannot be withdrawn because the decision is already final.

## Applying again after a rejection or decline

There is no waiting period on the portal: an applicant whose application was rejected by an officer or declined at pre-qualification may submit a new application straight away. A new application is assessed on its own figures, so reapplying with the same information will normally give the same result. It is more useful to first address the reasons given, for example by lowering the requested amount, reducing existing debts, improving the credit score over time, or choosing a product the applicant is eligible for, and then apply again.

## Having more than one application at the same time

An applicant may hold several applications at once, including for different products or for different businesses. Each application is pre-qualified on its own figures; the rules do not add up the applicant's other applications. A loan officer reviewing one application may still take the applicant's other open or recently approved applications into account when judging overall affordability, and repeated applications for the same purpose should be explained in the loan purpose.

## Choosing the right product for the applicant's situation

The Personal Loan on this portal is for salaried applicants only. Self-employed individuals, proprietors, partnerships, LLPs and private limited companies should apply through the Business Loan instead, which is designed for self-employed applicants and businesses in manufacturing, trading or services. A Credit Card is suited to regular spending rather than a one-off large expense. Choosing a product the applicant is not eligible for leads to a pre-qualification failure on a hard requirement, which an appeal is unlikely to change.

# Appealing a failed pre-qualification result

> **Provenance note:** LoanMitra portal policy for a student demonstration portal; not HDFC Bank policy.

## When an appeal or reconsideration request can be made

An applicant whose application failed pre-qualification can ask for it to be reconsidered by filing an appeal from the application page. An appeal is available only for a pre-qualification failure; an application already rejected by a loan officer cannot be appealed and the applicant would apply again instead. Filing an appeal moves the application to manual review, where a loan officer reads the appeal together with the application and documents. An appeal is a request for human review, not an automatic approval, and the officer may still reject the application.

## What a strong appeal contains

The appeal is a written explanation of between 20 and 2,000 characters. A useful appeal names the specific rule that failed, explains why the figure used was wrong, outdated or does not show the full picture, and points to the document that proves it. Good examples are an income that has recently increased with a new salary slip to show it, existing loans that have since been closed with closure letters, a credit score that has improved with a recent report, or a business that has operated longer than the form recorded. An appeal that only repeats the request or says the money is urgent gives the officer nothing new to assess. Appeals about hard eligibility requirements, such as employment type or age, rarely succeed unless the information on the form was entered incorrectly.

## How an appeal is handled

Appealed applications enter the same officer queue as other manual reviews. The officer sees the original rule results and the applicant's written appeal, checks the evidence in the uploaded documents, and records a written reason for the final decision. The applicant is emailed when an officer starts the review and again when the decision is made.

# Written reasons for decisions and fair treatment

> **Provenance note:** LoanMitra portal policy for a student demonstration portal; not HDFC Bank policy. It follows the principle in the Reserve Bank of India's Fair Practices Code that lenders convey the main reasons for rejecting a loan application in writing.

## Every decision carries a written reason

When an application is declined, the applicant is told why in writing. A pre-qualification failure is emailed with each rule that failed and the figure that caused it. A loan officer's approval or rejection must include a written note, and the reason is shown on the application page and sent to the applicant by email. An applicant who does not understand the reason for a rejection can ask the assistant to explain it from the application record.

## What a good rejection reason says

A rejection reason should name the specific criteria that were not met and the figures involved, in plain language the applicant can act on, for example that fixed obligations came to 74% of net monthly income against an allowed 50%. It should avoid vague phrases such as "does not meet internal norms" or "profile not suitable" on their own. Where possible it should say what would change the outcome, such as a lower loan amount, fewer existing EMIs or a longer business track record, and remind the applicant that they may apply again and how to raise a complaint if they believe the decision was wrong.

## Fair and consistent treatment

Decisions are based only on the applicant's financial information, documents and the product rules. An applicant's gender, caste, religion or other personal characteristics are never a reason for a decision. Similar applications should receive similar outcomes; when a loan officer decides two comparable borderline applications differently, the written notes should explain what was different, such as verified income, documented closure of existing loans, or a mismatch found in the documents.

# Affordability, FOIR, existing EMIs and debt consolidation

> **Provenance note:** LoanMitra portal policy for a student demonstration portal; not HDFC Bank policy. The 50% obligation limit is a portal threshold, not a published HDFC Bank figure.

## What FOIR means and how it is calculated

FOIR, the fixed obligations to income ratio, is the share of an applicant's net monthly income that already goes to fixed loan repayments. It is a debt-to-income measure used to judge whether a new repayment is affordable. For a Personal Loan, the portal adds the applicant's existing monthly EMIs to the estimated EMI of the new loan and divides by net monthly income; the result must be 50% or less. The new loan's EMI is estimated at 12% per annum over 36 months. For a Credit Card, only existing EMIs are counted against income, because a card has no fixed instalment. For example, an applicant earning ₹70,000 a month with ₹25,000 of existing EMIs who asks for ₹8 lakh has an estimated new EMI of about ₹26,571, so total obligations are about ₹51,571, or 74% of income, which is above the limit.

## Why existing EMIs are counted for a debt consolidation loan

A debt consolidation loan is taken to repay several existing loans or card balances with a single new loan. At the time of submission those older loans are still running, so the automated affordability rule counts both the existing EMIs and the new EMI, and a consolidation application can fail the FOIR rule even though the borrower plans to close the old loans. The rule cannot know that the old loans will actually be repaid, because that only happens after the new loan is paid out.

## How a loan officer assesses a consolidation application

When the loan purpose is debt consolidation and the application reaches a loan officer, the officer may assess affordability on the position after consolidation, counting only the EMIs that will remain plus the new EMI. In the example above, if all ₹25,000 of existing EMIs are closed, obligations fall to about ₹26,571, or 38% of income, which is within the limit. The officer should accept this only with evidence: a list of the loans to be closed with their outstanding amounts, current statements or foreclosure letters from those lenders, and agreement that the closures will be completed from the new loan, with closure letters provided after disbursal. If the new loan is smaller than the debts to be cleared, only the loans it fully repays should be removed from the calculation. The automated score itself does not change; the officer's written note records how affordability was judged.

## Ways an applicant can bring obligations within the limit

An applicant whose obligations are above 50% of income can request a smaller loan amount, close or prepay smaller existing loans before applying, or show additional regular income with documents. A longer repayment period lowers the monthly instalment of a real loan, but the automated rule always estimates the new EMI over 36 months, so a request for a longer tenure is considered by the loan officer rather than by the score.

## Affordability for a business loan

For a Business Loan, affordability is checked differently. The estimated EMI of the requested amount, at 12% per annum over 36 months, is compared with the monthly EMI the applicant says the business can pay, and annual revenue should be at least twice the requested amount. Both figures are self-reported, so the loan officer compares them with bank statement credits, GST returns or income tax returns before relying on them.

# Document checks before an officer decision

> **Provenance note:** LoanMitra portal guidance for loan officers on a student demonstration portal; not HDFC Bank's verification procedure.

## Which figures need proof

Everything an applicant types into the form is self-reported until a document confirms it. Before approving, a loan officer checks the figures the decision depends on: net monthly income, existing EMIs, credit score, employer or business name, years of employment or years in business, and for a business, annual revenue and the monthly EMI it can afford. A figure that cannot be matched to any document should be treated with caution, and the officer may ask the applicant for a further document before deciding.

## Checking a bank statement

A bank statement should cover the most recent six months and be in the applicant's or the business's own name. For a salaried applicant, the officer checks that the monthly salary credits match the declared net income and come from the stated employer. For a business, the officer compares the total business credits over the period with the declared annual revenue. In both cases the officer looks for regular EMI, ECS or NACH debits and checks that they match the existing EMIs declared on the form, since an undeclared EMI raises the real obligation ratio. Bounced cheques, returned EMI debits or frequent negative balances are warning signs of repayment stress. Large cash deposits that do not fit the applicant's pattern, or a balance that appears just before the application, should be explained. The average balance gives a rough sense of the buffer the applicant keeps.

## Checking the other documents

The name and date of birth on the Photo ID should match the application. A salary slip should be recent, show the same employer and a net pay consistent with the bank credits. A business registration certificate shows the legal form of the business and its registration date, which confirms how long it has operated. A GST certificate, GST returns or income tax returns, where provided, support the declared revenue. The portal records a fingerprint (SHA-256 hash) of every uploaded file and checks it on each download; a file that fails this check has changed since upload and must not be relied on.

## When the documents do not match the form

If a document contradicts the form, for example income lower than declared or an EMI that was not mentioned, the officer should judge the application on the verified figures rather than the declared ones. The officer may reject with a written reason that names the mismatch, or ask the applicant for an explanation before deciding. The reason for the final decision, including any mismatch found, is recorded in the officer's note.

# How loan officers review borderline applications

> **Provenance note:** LoanMitra portal guidance for loan officers on a student demonstration portal; not HDFC Bank's credit policy.

## Taking an application from the queue

Loan officers work from a shared queue of applications that passed pre-qualification or need manual review. An officer claims an application before reviewing it, which stops other officers from working on the same file. A claim that has not been acted on for an hour returns to the queue so another officer can take it over. An officer can never review or decide their own application.

## Reading a borderline application

A manual review usually means one scored rule failed while the rest of the profile passed, most often a credit score just below 650 or obligations slightly above 50% of income. The officer looks at the application as a whole rather than at the single failed rule. Considerations that weigh in favour include a credit score close to the threshold, income or revenue well above the minimum, a long and stable employment or business history, low existing obligations, a modest amount relative to income, and figures that are fully confirmed by documents. Considerations that weigh against include a score far below the threshold, obligations well above the limit, recent missed or bounced repayments, a very short track record, several recent applications for credit, and any mismatch between the form and the documents.

## Information that does not change the score

Product preferences, such as the facility type, tenure, industry, balance transfer, card variant, add-on card or Credit Protect, are recorded to help the officer and the assistant understand what the applicant wants. They are never part of the pre-qualification score. The HDFC Bank Business Loan offered through this portal is unsecured, so when a business applicant says collateral is available, the officer notes it, but it does not change the automated result.

## Recording the decision

The officer approves or rejects with a written note that states the main reason. For a borderline application that is approved, the note should say which considerations outweighed the failed rule and which documents confirmed them. For a rejection, the note should follow the portal's standard for written reasons. The AI-generated case summary can help an officer read the rule results quickly, but the officer must check it against the application, because it can be wrong and it never makes the decision.

# Privacy, chat conversations, documents and the AI assistant

> **Provenance note:** LoanMitra portal policy for a student demonstration portal; not HDFC Bank policy.

## Who can see an applicant's conversations with the assistant

Conversations with the LoanMitra Assistant are private to the person who had them. They are saved so the assistant can remember earlier messages in the same conversation, but no screen in the portal shows a borrower's chat history to loan officers or admins, and chat messages are never part of the application an officer reviews. What decides an application is the information in the form, the uploaded documents and any written appeal, not anything said to the assistant.

## Personal identifiers are removed from messages

Before a message reaches the AI model, the portal removes personal identifiers such as Aadhaar numbers, PAN, card numbers, mobile numbers and email addresses and replaces them with a placeholder, and the user is told that this happened. The question is still answered. Applicants never need to share these numbers in a chat; identity is checked through the documents uploaded with an application.

## What the assistant can and cannot do

The assistant answers questions about loan and credit card policy from published HDFC Bank and Reserve Bank of India documents and this portal policy, explains the user's own applications, and calculates EMIs. It cites the passage each answer comes from and says so when the documents do not cover a question. It does not approve or reject applications, change a score or give personal financial advice. A borrower's assistant only ever sees that borrower's own applications. A loan officer can use the assistant on an application only after claiming it for review, and an admin on any application; each such use is recorded in the audit log. When a loan officer asks about an application, the assistant explains its figures and lists the policy that supports or weighs against it, but it never recommends approving or rejecting, because that decision is the officer's. It can be wrong, so important decisions should be checked against the official documents. Borrowers have a daily AI usage limit of 50,000 tokens, which resets at midnight Indian Standard Time; loan officers and admins have no limit.

## Who can see uploaded documents and how long they are kept

Uploaded documents can be opened by the applicant, by loan officers reviewing the application and by admins. They stay with the application record after it is approved, rejected or withdrawn, because lending records must be retained; they are not deleted when an application is declined or cancelled. For the same reason, an account that has any loan applications on record cannot be deleted. An account with no applications can be deleted by its owner from the Security page.

# Credit Protect choice and credit score basics

> **Provenance note:** LoanMitra portal policy for a student demonstration portal; not HDFC Bank policy. Insurance terms are set by the insurer, not by this portal.

## Choosing Credit Protect with a personal loan

Credit Protect is optional loan protection insurance that a personal loan applicant can choose on the application form. Choosing it or declining it is recorded as a preference only: it never affects the pre-qualification score, the decision or the eligibility of the application. The portal does not quote the premium, the cover or the exclusions, and it does not add the premium to the EMI it estimates. Those terms are set out in the insurer's own documents, which are shared with the borrower at the time the loan is sanctioned, and the borrower should read them before agreeing.

## Credit scores in the pre-qualification rules

The portal uses the credit score the applicant enters on the form. A score of 650 or above passes the credit score rule. A score from 550 to 649 fails that scored rule, which usually leads to manual review if the rest of the application is strong. A score below 550 is a hard requirement failure for every product. An applicant who has never had a loan or credit card may have a low score, or no score at all, simply because there is too little repayment history to measure; this is often called a thin credit file and is different from a record of missed payments.

## How long a credit score takes to change

A credit score is built from the repayment information that lenders report to credit bureaus, so it changes gradually rather than overnight. Paying every EMI and card bill in full and on time, keeping card balances low compared with the limit, avoiding many applications for credit in a short period and correcting errors on the credit report all help, and improvements typically appear over the following months as new repayment records are reported. A score that improves after an application has been submitted does not change that application's stored result; the applicant can apply again with the new score.
