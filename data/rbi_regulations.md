# RBI FAQ - Credit Card and Debit Card Issuance and Conduct

> **Provenance note:** This is a source-linked, retrieval-friendly text extract/summary prepared from the official page/document identified below. It is not a replacement for the complete official original. Use the official source URL for the complete document and check for amendments before relying on numerical terms.

## Key FAQ answers captured from the official RBI page

- **Unsolicited cards:** Issuers are prohibited from issuing a credit card without prior and explicit customer consent. If an unsolicited card is received, the customer should not activate it or give activation consent.
- **No activation within 30 days:** If a card remains unactivated for more than 30 days from issuance, the issuer must seek OTP-based consent. If activation consent is not received, the account is to be closed without cost within seven working days of seeking confirmation, with an intimation to the customer.
- **Partial payment:** If the total amount due is not cleared by the due date, the interest-free period is lost. Interest may be charged from the transaction date on the outstanding amount (adjusted for payments, refunds and reversals), rather than on the total amount due.
- **Late fees:** Late-payment fees and related delay charges are to be levied on the outstanding amount after the due date, adjusted for payments/refunds/reversed transactions, not on the total amount due.
- **Unpaid taxes/charges:** Issuers must not capitalise unpaid taxes, levies or charges by imposing interest or other charges on those unpaid items.
- **Over-limit facility:** Going above the sanctioned credit limit requires prior explicit cardholder consent. The cardholder must have an option to enable or disable the facility; without consent, over-limit usage and over-limit charges cannot be imposed.
- **Closure:** Blocking/deactivation is different from account closure. A closure request is to be honoured within seven working days, subject to the FAQ's handling of outstanding dues.
- **Escalation:** The customer should first approach the card issuer. If it fails to respond within 30 days, rejects the complaint, or the customer is dissatisfied with the resolution, the customer may lodge a complaint with RBI through its complaint platform under the applicable Ombudsman Scheme.

This is a concise factual extract, not the full FAQ. Use the official FAQ and Master Direction for complete wording and exceptions.

# RBI Directions - Reset of Floating Interest Rate on EMI-Based Personal Loans

> **Provenance note:** This is a source-linked, retrieval-friendly text extract/summary prepared from the official page/document identified below. It is not a replacement for the complete official original. Use the official source URL for the complete document and check for amendments before relying on numerical terms.

These RBI directions address equated-instalment personal loans with floating interest rates. The source page indicates that it was updated as on 1 October 2025.

Key borrower-facing requirements described include:

- At sanction, the regulated entity should explain how benchmark-rate changes can affect the EMI and/or loan tenor; subsequent increases in EMI/tenor should be communicated promptly.
- At a rate reset, the lender may, at its option and under its Board-approved policy, provide a choice to switch to a fixed rate.
- Borrowers should be offered choices between increasing EMI, extending the tenor, combining the two, and prepaying in part or full; any foreclosure/prepayment charges remain subject to applicable RBI instructions.
- Switching charges and related service/administrative costs should be transparently disclosed.
- Tenor extension should not cause negative amortisation.
- Borrowers should have access to a quarterly statement that at minimum lists principal and interest recovered to date, EMI, number of EMIs remaining, and annualised interest/APR for the full tenor.

These directions are not general approval eligibility rules and do not apply to all loan types. Check the live RBI page and product contract for scope and exceptions.

# RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme - Customer Complaint Escalation

> **Provenance note:** This is a source-linked, retrieval-friendly text extract/summary prepared from the official page/document identified below. It is not a replacement for the complete official original. Use the official source URL for the complete document and check for amendments before relying on numerical terms.

The Reserve Bank - Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2021 consolidates RBI's prior ombudsman schemes into a unified mechanism for eligible complaints about services provided by regulated entities (banks, NBFCs, payment system operators). This covers loan-related complaints including loan application rejection, as well as credit card and other banking service complaints.

**For any banking complaint (loans, credit cards, account services):** A customer must first raise the complaint with the regulated entity (the bank or lender) directly. If the entity does not respond within 30 days, rejects the complaint wholly or partly, or the customer is not satisfied with the response/resolution, the customer may escalate to the RBI through the Complaint Management System at https://cms.rbi.org.in, subject to the scheme's conditions.

Chatbot answers should tell users to preserve complaint/reference numbers and check the current scheme and eligibility requirements. Do not claim that every banking dispute automatically qualifies or that RBI guarantees a particular outcome.

# RBI Circular - Key Facts Statement (KFS) for Loans and Advances

> **Provenance note:** This is a source-linked, retrieval-friendly text extract/summary prepared from the official page/document identified below. It is not a replacement for the complete official original. Use the official source URL for the complete document and check for amendments before relying on numerical terms.

## Scope

RBI's KFS circular harmonises key-fact disclosures for retail and MSME term-loan products extended by regulated entities. It is intended to help borrowers make informed decisions before signing a loan contract.

## Requirements highlighted in the circular

- A regulated entity must provide prospective borrowers a standardised Key Facts Statement in a language the borrower understands, and explain its contents.
- The KFS must have a unique proposal number and a validity period of at least three working days for loans with a tenor of seven days or more; for shorter tenors, at least one working day.
- The KFS must include an APR computation sheet and the amortisation schedule for the loan.
- APR is the annual cost of credit, including interest and other charges associated with the credit facility.
- Third-party charges recovered by the lender (such as insurance or legal charges) must be included in APR and disclosed separately; related receipts/documents must be provided within a reasonable time.
- Charges not disclosed in the KFS may not be imposed during the loan term without the borrower's explicit consent.
- The KFS must be included as a summary box in the loan agreement.
- The circular expressly exempts credit-card receivables; the circular is not the credit-card rules source.
- All new retail and MSME term loans sanctioned on or after 1 October 2024 are required to comply with the instructions.

# RBI Circular - Fair Lending Practice: Penal Charges in Loan Accounts

> **Provenance note:** This is a source-linked, retrieval-friendly text extract/summary prepared from the official page/document identified below. It is not a replacement for the complete official original. Use the official source URL for the complete document and check for amendments before relying on numerical terms.

**Terminology note:** "Penal interest" is the legacy term; the RBI's August 2023 circular replaced it with "penal charges." When borrowers ask about "penal interest," they are asking about what this circular now calls penal charges.

The RBI's Fair Lending Practice circular states that penal charges (formerly called penal interest) for non-compliance with material loan terms are intended to promote credit discipline, not to act as a revenue-enhancement component on top of the contracted interest rate.

Key points for a chatbot:

- The quantum and reason for penal charges should be clearly disclosed to the borrower in the loan agreement and, as applicable, the Most Important Terms and Conditions / Key Facts Statement, as well as on the regulated entity's website.
- Penal charges should be reasonable and commensurate with the non-compliance of material terms, and should not be discriminatory within the same loan/product category.
- Penal charges should not be capitalised into additional interest (that is, no interest should be calculated on penal charges themselves), while ordinary compounding of interest in the loan account is not affected by this statement.
- The RBI's 29 December 2023 circular extended the implementation date for fresh loans to 1 April 2024, with existing accounts switching at review/renewal on or after 1 April 2024 and not later than 30 June 2024.

Read the original circular and its FAQ for complete scope, exclusions and interpretation. Do not replace a bank's published fee schedule with an invented standard penalty rate.

# RBI Directions - Release of Property Documents After Loan Repayment/Settlement

> **Provenance note:** This is a source-linked, retrieval-friendly text extract/summary prepared from the official page/document identified below. It is not a replacement for the complete official original. Use the official source URL for the complete document and check for amendments before relying on numerical terms.

The RBI directions say regulated entities must release original movable/immovable property documents and remove charges registered with any registry within 30 days after full repayment/settlement of the loan account.

The borrower must be allowed to collect the documents from the branch/outlet that serviced the loan or another office where the documents are available, according to their preference. The sanction letter should state the timeline and place of return.

If the lender is responsible for delay beyond 30 days, it must communicate its reasons and compensate the borrower at ₹5,000 for each day of delay. If original documents are lost or damaged, the lender must assist in obtaining duplicate/certified copies and bear associated costs, in addition to applicable compensation; an additional 30 days is available for this replacement process before the delayed-period penalty is calculated.

These provisions relate to original property documents/registered charges after full repayment or settlement; do not confuse them with a general rule that applies to every unsecured personal loan.
